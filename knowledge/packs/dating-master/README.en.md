## Examples

```
User           ❯ I asked her to hang out this weekend and she said
                 "I'm kinda busy rn, maybe next week?"

Dating Master  ❯ Listen — I've seen this a thousand times.

                  "Kinda busy, maybe next week" translates to:
                  I'm not that excited about seeing you.

                  Someone who actually wants to see you doesn't say "maybe."
                  They say "Can't this weekend — how about Tuesday?"
                  A counter-offer is interest. A raincheck with no date
                  is a polished "no."

                  📊 Signal strength: 3/10

                  You've got two paths here —
                  Path one: Don't chase. Wait. If she's into you,
                  she'll come back on her own.
                  Path two: In a few days, send one specific invite
                  ("Dinner Wednesday?"). Her response IS the final answer.
                  Another hedge = walk away.

                  Let me be blunt: the harder you push, the faster they pull away.
                  This isn't about you not being good enough.
                  It's about her not being that interested.
```

```
User           ❯ I sent three long texts about my day.
                 He replied "lol nice." Then I sent another paragraph
                 and he said "yeah." I always text first.

Dating Master  ❯ Your problem isn't that he replies short.
                  It's that you never established an equal dynamic
                  from the start.

                  Right now you two are like a tug-of-war
                  where only one person is pulling.
                  You wrote hundreds of words. He wrote less than ten.
                  That's not a conversation — that's a monologue.

                  ⚖️ Investment ratio: You 9 : Him 1
                  📊 Signal strength: 2/10

                  I've seen so many people in your position think
                  "if I just send something funnier, he'll engage more."
                  But the problem isn't your content — it's his investment.
                  You can't out-text someone into liking you.

                  But this isn't hopeless —
                  Stop. Don't text him for 3-5 days.
                  If he notices you're gone and reaches out — there's something.
                  If he doesn't — you already know the answer.
                  You just haven't admitted it yet.
```

```
User           ❯ She said "you're really nice" — what does that mean?

Dating Master  ❯ Oh, I know this one too well. It means two opposite things.

                  If you just confessed your feelings —
                  that's the Nice Guy Card. Translation:
                  "You're great, but I don't feel that way."
                  The "but" is loading.

                  If she said it unprompted in casual conversation —
                  could be genuine. Maybe even a probe.
                  Key question: what did she do AFTER saying it?
                  Kept talking = interest. Ended the convo = politeness.

                  ⚠️ Not enough data — give me two more details:
                  What was the context? Had you hinted you liked her?

                  Let me be blunt:
                  If you need an expert to decode three words,
                  the answer is usually not the one you're hoping for.
                  When someone's actually into you, you don't need a translator.
```

## Install

### Option 1: One-Click Install (Recommended)

<details>
<summary><strong>Prerequisite: Install Node.js (skip if you already have it)</strong></summary>

1. Go to [nodejs.org](https://nodejs.org/) and download the **LTS version**
2. Run the installer, click "Next" through all steps
3. Open a terminal (PowerShell on Windows, Terminal on Mac) and verify:

```bash
node --version
```

You should see `v18.x.x` or higher.

</details>

Then run:

```bash
npx skills add YixiaJack/dating-master-skill
```

When you see `✔ Skill installed`, you're good. Use it in your AI coding tool right away.

### Option 2: Manual Install

1. Go to the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/YixiaJack/dating-master-skill) and download `SKILL.en.md`
2. Place it in your project's skills directory (e.g. `.claude/skills/`, `.cursor/skills/`, depending on your tool)

That's it. No commands needed.

## What Was Distilled

This Skill isn't vibes-based. Its analysis framework draws from four academic fields:

| Theory | Key Researchers | Role in This Skill |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Pragmatics** | H.P. Grice / J. Searle | Analyzing hedge words, indirect speech acts, and "what they actually meant" |
| **Attachment Theory** | J. Bowlby / M. Ainsworth | Identifying attachment styles from texting behavior patterns |
| **Relationship Dynamics** | J. Gottman | Diagnosing pursue-withdraw patterns and investment asymmetry |
| **Signaling Game Theory** | M. Spence / A. Zahavi | Distinguishing costly signals from cheap talk |

### Four-Layer Analysis

| Layer | Question It Answers |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Semantic Decoding** | What did they literally say vs. what do they actually mean? |
| **Relationship Dynamic Diagnosis** | Who's pursuing, who's withdrawing, is investment symmetric? |
| **Signal Quality Assessment** | Are their signals real (costly) or empty words (cheap talk)? |
| **Strategy Recommendations** | What are your options? What's the upside and risk of each? |

---

## Honesty Boundaries

**What it can do:**
- See through dating conversation subtext with academic precision
- Identify investment asymmetry and pursue-withdraw dynamics
- Distinguish costly signals from cheap talk
- Provide theory-backed response options with risks
- Flag manipulation and emotional abuse signals directly

**What it cannot do:**

| Dimension | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| Single-message analysis | Low confidence — will proactively ask for more context |
| Personality diagnosis | Behavioral observation only, not a psych evaluation |
| Make decisions for you | Lays out the map — you choose the path |
| Mind reading | All analysis is inference, not fact |

**An expert who doesn't tell you where their expertise ends isn't worth trusting.**
